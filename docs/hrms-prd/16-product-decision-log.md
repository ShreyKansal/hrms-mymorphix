### D-001: Treat effective-dating as foundational, not a later-phase enhancement
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** Deciding how to model organisational/employment history for [05-organisation-data-model.md](05-organisation-data-model.md). **Alternatives considered:** (a) current-state-only schema with audit-log-based history reconstruction, (b) full effective-dating from day one via the Employment Assignment pattern. **Selected:** (b). **Rationale:** retrofitting effective-dating after a current-state-only MVP ships is one of the most expensive re-architectures possible for an HRMS, and the brief explicitly requires historical-hierarchy-preservation and point-in-time queries as core capabilities, not nice-to-haves. **Trade-offs:** higher upfront data-model complexity and implementation cost in the Foundation Release. **Impacted modules:** 1, 2, 5, 6, 9, 15, all others indirectly. **Revisit condition:** none anticipated — this is treated as a structural commitment, not a reversible experiment.

### D-002: Compensation is a separate effective-dated entity from Employment Assignment
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** Modelling salary changes ([05-organisation-data-model.md](05-organisation-data-model.md) §8). **Alternatives:** fold compensation fields into Employment Assignment. **Selected:** separate entity. **Rationale:** compensation changes independently of org-assignment changes often enough (annual increment with no transfer; lateral transfer with no pay change) that conflating them would force artificial records. **Trade-offs:** two effective-dated entities to keep synchronised conceptually, slightly more query complexity for "full picture as of date X." **Impacted modules:** 1, 6. **Revisit condition:** none anticipated.

### D-003: Product name is a placeholder ("Project Meridian"), not a real proposal
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** [03-product-vision.md](03-product-vision.md) needed a stable internal reference name across 25+ documents. **Alternatives:** leave "[Product Name]" as a literal blank throughout. **Selected:** a clearly-labelled placeholder. **Rationale:** improves document readability without pretending to have done brand/trademark work out of scope for this PRD phase. **Trade-offs:** risk that the placeholder calicifies into the real name through repeated use (explicitly flagged as OQ-19). **Impacted modules:** all documents referencing the product by name. **Revisit condition:** the moment an actual brand name is chosen, a global find-replace should happen, and this entry updated.

### D-004: India-first, architecture-ready-for-multi-country (not multi-country-at-launch)
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** [03-product-vision.md](03-product-vision.md) target-market definition, informed by competitor research showing Darwinbox as the only researched competitor with confirmed multi-country statutory depth. **Alternatives:** (a) India-only with no multi-country consideration in the data model, (b) build multi-country payroll into MVP, (c) India-first GTM with multi-country-ready architecture ([05-organisation-data-model.md](05-organisation-data-model.md)'s Legal Entity/data-residency provisions). **Selected:** (c). **Rationale:** multi-country payroll at MVP is high-complexity/high-risk for a first release already carrying significant Payroll-module risk (D-001-adjacent); but (a) would risk an expensive later re-architecture given Darwinbox's evidenced multi-country moat. **Trade-offs:** some upfront modelling cost (reserved fields, Legal Entity abstraction) for a capability not used at launch. **Impacted modules:** 5, 6, all Enterprise-Release-tagged capabilities. **Revisit condition:** if actual near-term expansion targets a specific country (OQ-18), the specific country's compliance metadata needs should be scoped concretely at that point, not left generic.

### D-005: Statutory rules are versioned configuration, not hard-coded logic
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** Module 6 §10, directly responding to the "regulatory change lag" risk named in [04-personas-and-roles.md](04-personas-and-roles.md) Persona 7. **Alternatives:** hard-code PF/ESI/PT/TDS rules in application logic, updated via normal release cycles. **Selected:** versioned, effective-dated configuration layer. **Rationale:** statutory changes (PT slabs, PF ceiling, tax slabs, labour codes) happen on a schedule this product doesn't control, and coupling them to a software-release cadence is both slower and riskier than necessary. **Trade-offs:** more upfront architecture investment in a configuration engine rather than simpler hard-coded rules. **Impacted modules:** 6, 10 (mandatory-training applicability rules follow the same pattern). **Revisit condition:** none anticipated — this is treated as correct regardless of implementation cost, given the alternative's demonstrated risk.

### D-006: Payroll rollback terminology is deliberately honest about its limits
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** Module 6 §7.2 — designing the "what happens when a payroll error is caught late" workflow. **Alternatives:** market/design a unified "rollback" capability implying full reversibility. **Selected:** explicit UI/product distinction between pre-disbursal rollback (a genuine, clean undo) and post-disbursal correction (a financial-recovery process, not a system undo). **Rationale:** overpromising reversibility after real fund movement is both a UX-honesty issue and a potential legal-liability issue if customers rely on a false expectation — flagged as a specific, considered risk (Module 6 §27) rather than a detail to gloss over. **Trade-offs:** a less simple, less marketable single "rollback" story. **Impacted modules:** 6, 15 (full-and-final settlement shares this concern). **Revisit condition:** none anticipated.

### D-007: AI capabilities require mandatory human review for every consequential action
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** Module 25, directly responding to the Darwinbox-evidenced "AI-forward marketing vs. still-basic-automation" credibility gap found in Phase 2 research. **Alternatives:** pursue more autonomous AI-agent orchestration (the Darwinbox "Super Agent"/Rippling AI-workflow-generation pattern found in competitor research). **Selected:** conservative, human-gated design — AI never directly executes a consequential business action. **Rationale:** the specific, evidenced risk of AI-forward marketing outpacing delivered reliability is a named competitor weakness this product should not replicate; bias/fairness risk in recruitment/performance/attrition capabilities is severe enough to warrant hard gates. **Trade-offs:** a less flashy, less "AI-native" market position relative to Darwinbox/Rippling's marketing. **Impacted modules:** 25, and every module Module 25 assists. **Revisit condition:** could be revisited for specific, narrow, well-evidenced-safe capabilities as the product matures and gains a track record — not a blanket reversal, a capability-by-capability reassessment.

### D-008: IT/System Administrator roles are structurally separated from general HR-content access
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** [04-personas-and-roles.md](04-personas-and-roles.md) Persona 11, carried through Modules 21/22/23. **Alternatives:** a single broad "Administrator" role bundling system configuration and HR-data access, matching a common (if risky) real-world pattern. **Selected:** distinct, independently-grantable permission domains. **Rationale:** a common real-world anti-pattern is over-granting IT admins broad HR-data visibility just because they hold an "Administrator" label — this product deliberately designs against that. **Trade-offs:** slightly more configuration complexity when setting up IT Administrator accounts (two grants instead of one bundled one). **Impacted modules:** 21, 22, 23. **Revisit condition:** none anticipated — this is treated as a correctness principle, not a convenience trade-off to reconsider.

### D-009: Mobile scope is deliberately partial, not full desktop parity
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** Module 24, [03-product-vision.md](03-product-vision.md) Product Principle 10, directly responding to the near-universal mobile-parity-gap finding in Phase 2 research (5 of 8 competitors). **Alternatives:** commit to full feature parity as a differentiator (the one gap BambooHR mostly avoids). **Selected:** deliberate scoping to high-frequency, appropriately-low-risk actions, with high-blast-radius actions explicitly excluded from mobile by design (payroll processing, permission configuration, bulk operations). **Rationale:** every competitor's mobile gap is partly a *reasonable* scoping choice (Rippling itself can't run payroll from mobile) — reframing "gap" as "considered boundary" for the high-risk actions, while still closing the gap that matters (high-frequency employee/manager actions). **Trade-offs:** cannot claim "full mobile parity" as a marketing point. **Impacted modules:** 24, and every module's own §20 mobile-requirements section. **Revisit condition:** mobile scope should expand based on validated usage data (Module 24 §10), not by default parity-chasing.

### D-010: The workflow engine (Module 17) and permission model (Module 21) are shared infrastructure, not per-module reinvention
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** Designing how approval logic and access control are implemented consistently across 25 modules. **Alternatives:** let each module implement its own approval/permission logic as needed. **Selected:** every module routes through the same two shared engines. **Rationale:** directly responds to the "rigid, hard-wired" competitor complaint pattern (Keka, RazorpayX) — a single, well-designed, genuinely configurable engine avoids both the inconsistency risk of per-module reinvention and the risk of each module's approval logic independently degrading into the rigid pattern being avoided. **Trade-offs:** Module 17/21 become high-leverage, high-risk dependencies that must be designed exceptionally well up front, since flaws propagate everywhere. **Impacted modules:** effectively all 25. **Revisit condition:** none anticipated — this is a foundational architecture commitment.

### D-011: ESS/MSS (Module 16) is a pure aggregation layer with no owned data
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** Designing the employee/manager self-service experience. **Alternatives:** let Module 16 cache/duplicate data from other modules for rendering-performance convenience. **Selected:** Module 16 reads/writes only through owning modules in real time, never its own parallel data store. **Rationale:** directly guards against the Zoho-style one-way-sync/data-drift failure mode this whole PRD is designed to avoid as its central competitive differentiation ([03-product-vision.md](03-product-vision.md)). **Trade-offs:** potentially more real-time query load on owning modules than a cached-aggregation approach would produce. **Impacted modules:** 16, and every module it aggregates. **Revisit condition:** if performance testing (Phase 12) reveals genuine problems, a caching layer could be introduced — but must preserve the "no second source of truth" guarantee (e.g., a short-TTL cache with clear invalidation, not a drift-prone independent store).

### D-012: Bulk-select and bulk-action UI must be custom-built on top of Atlaskit's table primitives
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** [00-existing-system-audit.md](00-existing-system-audit.md) §6 finding that neither `@atlaskit/table` nor `@atlaskit/dynamic-table` ships row-selection/bulk-action support. **Alternatives:** avoid bulk actions where possible, or build a bespoke non-Atlaskit table component. **Selected:** build bulk-select/action as a consistent pattern layered on top of the existing primitives, applied identically across every module needing it (per each module's own §17). **Rationale:** bulk actions are explicitly required across nearly every module (per the brief), and inconsistent per-module implementations would undermine the design-system-consistency goal ("use the existing design system consistently"). **Trade-offs:** genuine build effort not covered by the design system out of the box. **Impacted modules:** 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 13, 14, 15, 20, 21 (every module with a §17 bulk-action requirement). **Revisit condition:** if Atlaskit ships native bulk-select support in a future version, re-evaluate whether to adopt it in place of the custom pattern.

### D-013: Drawer component is avoided; Modal or custom panels used instead
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** [00-existing-system-audit.md](00-existing-system-audit.md) §7 finding that `@atlaskit/drawer` carries an "intent-to-deprecate" status, with the component's own guidance suggesting Modal as an alternative. **Alternatives:** use Drawer anyway for slide-in panels given it technically still works. **Selected:** default to Modal for focused tasks, custom primitive-built panels for genuine slide-in needs; Drawer excluded from this product's approved-component list. **Rationale:** adopting a component the design system itself is deprecating would create technical debt from day one. **Trade-offs:** slightly more custom-build work for any genuine slide-in-panel need (e.g., a lightweight "quick view" pattern) that Drawer would have served conveniently. **Impacted modules:** [07-information-architecture.md](07-information-architecture.md) primarily, with downstream effect on any module needing overlay UI. **Revisit condition:** none anticipated unless Atlaskit reverses the deprecation.

### D-014: Gap-check against actual competitor feature coverage, post-25-module-draft
**Date:** 2026-07-23. **Context:** After completing all 25 originally-scoped modules, the user asked whether every module a real HRMS (Zoho, RazorpayX, etc.) might have was covered. Cross-checking the completed PRD against the actual feature sets documented in [01-market-research.md](01-market-research.md) surfaced several capabilities competitors ship as distinct modules that the original 25-module brief didn't name explicitly: POSH/Internal Committee case management, Benefits Administration, Compensation Management (bands/benchmarking, distinct from Payroll processing), contractor/gig-worker payroll, ESOP administration, statutory registers, and succession planning/skills taxonomy. **Alternatives considered:** (a) leave the 25-module scope as-is since it matched the original brief exactly, (b) add all 7 gaps as full new modules, (c) add the two highest-severity, most India-compliance-relevant gaps (POSH, Benefits) as full modules and fold the remaining lower-severity ones into existing modules' scope rather than standing up new module documents for them. **Selected:** (c), per explicit user direction. **Rationale:** POSH is a legally mandated process for any Indian employer with 10+ employees, with a confidentiality requirement stricter than anything else in the product, and its absence directly undercut this PRD's own "compliance-first" positioning — treating it as adequately covered by the generic Module 11/12 grievance/helpdesk machinery would have been a real compliance gap, not just a documentation gap. Benefits Administration is a distinct, separately-monetised module for 3 of the 8 competitors researched (Zoho, BambooHR, Keka) and doesn't map cleanly onto Module 6's salary-structuring concepts. Contractor payroll, ESOP, and statutory registers were folded into Module 6/Module 1's existing scope rather than new modules, since they're genuine extensions of payroll/employee-data processing, not distinct new workflows. Compensation Management (bands/benchmarking) and Succession Planning/Skills Taxonomy were assessed as lower-severity (Module 1's Grade/Band and Module 9/10's existing goal/skill-gap concepts already provide adequate coverage for MVP purposes) and are logged here as a deliberate scope decision rather than built out further at this time. **Trade-offs:** two new modules (26, 27) added after the original 25-module structure was already complete, requiring updates across the master PRD, roadmap, data model, and report catalogue to stay consistent — a real integration cost, accepted because the alternative (an undocumented gap in a compliance-first product) was worse. **Impacted modules:** new 26 (POSH), new 27 (Benefits Administration), amended 1 (ESOP data) and 6 (contractor payroll, ESOP tax treatment, statutory registers). **Revisit condition:** Compensation Management and Succession Planning/Skills Taxonomy should be revisited as candidate new modules if a specific customer/market signal (not just competitor feature-parity) makes the case — this was a "not yet justified," not a "never," decision.
